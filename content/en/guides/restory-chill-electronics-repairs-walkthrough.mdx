The confirmed ReStory: Chill Electronics Repairs walkthrough is a repeating shop loop: accept a customer device, disassemble it, clean it, replace faulty parts, reassemble it, and deliver the finished repair. You also manage money, use the Y2K-era browser to find spare parts, and handle both offline and online orders. The exact chapter order and unlock dates are **Unconfirmed**, so this page separates the reliable loop from details that still need a full-game check.

## Start with the repair-shop loop

The game places you behind an electronics repair counter in mid-2000s Tokyo. A job begins with a customer and a device, but the official material does not publish one universal first-day task list. Treat the first shift as an orientation to the shop, the workbench, customer conversations, and the repair sequence rather than relying on an unverified chapter guide.

<RepairStep number="01" title="Accept the device and read the request">
  Start from the customer request and the device that needs attention. The exact order of the first requests and the names of the opening tasks are **Unconfirmed**, but customer choices can affect the shop and the stories around it.
</RepairStep>

<RepairStep number="02" title="Disassemble before cleaning">
  Take the device apart so the relevant parts can be inspected and handled. The official description confirms disassembly as part of the repair flow, while the precise interaction order for every device remains **Unconfirmed**.
</RepairStep>

<RepairStep number="03" title="Clean and replace faulty parts">
  Clean the separated parts, identify the faulty component, and replace it with a suitable spare. Use the in-game browser and shop systems to search for supplies; the complete device-to-part catalog and all prices are **Unconfirmed**.
</RepairStep>

<RepairStep number="04" title="Reassemble and deliver">
  Rebuild the device and return the finished order. The game combines this hands-on work with money management, customer feedback, and story decisions, so a completed repair is also part of the wider shop progression.
</RepairStep>

## What to track between jobs

The shop is not only a sequence of workbench interactions. The confirmed systems include customer orders, funds, bills, licenses, tools, shop decoration, web searches, and online requests. The full release announcement also mentions new devices, upgradeable tools, store walls, shelves, decorations, and more characters, but it does not publish every unlock trigger.

Keep a simple note for each job: the device category, the customer request, the faulty part, the source used for the spare, and whether the order was offline or online. This creates a useful personal walkthrough without turning an unverified list into a universal route. The exact unlock day for each tool, license, device, or decoration is **Unconfirmed** and should be recorded from the full game before being added as a fixed table.

## Use the browser and online orders carefully

The Y2K-era web browser is a confirmed part of the game’s supply loop. It is used to hunt for spare parts, while online requests and email orders add another way to receive work. The achievement page confirms milestones for the first email order, 25 email orders, and 100 email orders, which makes online work worth tracking from the beginning.

Do not assume that every browser option has a known effect. The community discussion list includes a question about an Internet speed choice and difficulty, but the option values, refresh rules, cancellation rules, and story effects are **Unconfirmed**. The same caution applies to marketplace sourcing and whether a particular order can be repeated after delivery.

## Follow the story without losing the repair rhythm

ReStory has a branching storyline and multiple endings, and the official description says customer interaction can affect people’s lives and the shop. Dialogue choices are therefore part of progression, not just flavor text. The official examples include deciding whether to report a suspicious discovery on a former gangster’s phone and whether to help a lovestruck student propose.

For a first playthrough, record the date, customer context, and choice you made instead of copying a route from an early Demo. The exact number of endings, their formal names, their trigger conditions, and whether the shop remains playable after the final story event are **Unconfirmed**. Use the separate Endings page when those details have been verified in the full release.

## Assembly competitions and long-term progression

The achievement list confirms that device assembly competitions exist as a progression target. Promise of Akiba requires one win, Star of Akiba requires wins in three different device assembly competitions, and Legend of Akiba requires at least one win for each device type. The exact competition unlock points and the meaning of every device category are **Unconfirmed**.

The official release material describes a main story of at least 15 hours, with playtime varying by player. Branching choices, device customization, and online requests provide replay value, but they do not prove a fixed number of post-story activities. Keep those systems in your walkthrough as separate milestones until a stable full-release sequence is documented.
