## Strengths described by the official material

The repair loop has a clear tactile identity: disassemble an object, clean it, replace the faulty part, and put it back together. The official announcement material also highlights cleaning, a screwdriver, 2000s nostalgia, repair work, and customer stories as positive themes. These are the game’s intended strengths and can explain why the store appeals to cozy simulation players.

The shop layer adds money management, spare-part searches through a Y2K browser, offline and online orders, tools, decoration, and more devices in the full release. Branching customer choices and multiple endings give the workbench a narrative context. The main story is advertised as at least 15 hours, with playtime varying by player, while customization and online requests add replay value.

## Things to know before buying

Cleaning is a core action, so players who dislike repeated hands-on maintenance may find the loop repetitive. That is a caution based on a community discussion topic, not a conclusion about every player. Keep the statement qualified rather than presenting repetition as a universal defect.

Community topics also raise controller support, Steam Deck, ultrawide, and gadgets stuck in the sonic bath. These are useful issues to check, but individual posts do not prove that every copy has the problem or that the same workaround works on the current build. The detailed controller, resolution, and cleaning pages therefore mark unresolved behavior **Unconfirmed**.

The game fits players looking for a single-player, cozy electronics repair shop with a mid-2000s Tokyo setting.
