## Confirmed device families

The official Steam description gives the broadest reliable catalog structure: nostalgic game consoles, handhelds, phones, cameras, music players, and home appliances. These are categories rather than a complete list of in-game models. Use them as filters in a reference page, and do not turn a category into an invented device count.

The repair flow connects the device entry to a faulty component, cleaning stage, replacement spare, tool, and final assembly. The supplied sources do not publish every field for every device, so the catalog should leave unknown cells visibly marked **待确认**. This is especially important for marketplace devices and salvage parts, where the available source may change by version or shop state.

The official Demo announcement names Pokia N-Joy, Patento BS, and Palaloid. ## Recommended catalog fields

## How to use the catalog while playing

Start with the device category and the customer request, then note the part that the game identifies as faulty. Search the Y2K-era browser for the spare and record whether the order came from a web listing, an online request, a marketplace purchase, or another source. Do not infer that every marketplace device can be dismantled for a reliable spare-part supply; that behavior is **待确认**.

When the part is in hand, record the tool and the repair state rather than only the final result. This makes it possible to connect the parts catalog to the walkthrough, cleaning guide, Internet page, and Atari page without publishing unsupported prices or unlock dates. A source-backed catalog is more useful than a long list of names that cannot be checked in the full release.

## Atari entries need their own notes

The Atari announcement gives unusually specific teardown context: Atari 2600 and the CX40 joystick are described with four screws, a motherboard, joystick, upper and lower shells, a red button, and power cables. The game simplifies the motherboard-to-power-cable connection for playability. These are official in-game teardown notes, not a complete parts catalog for every Atari device.

Atari Lynx belongs in a preview or pending row until its full-release availability is checked. The same rule applies to any device that appears in an announcement but has not been confirmed in the current build. Do not provide real-world high-voltage teardown advice on this page; it is an in-game reference only.
